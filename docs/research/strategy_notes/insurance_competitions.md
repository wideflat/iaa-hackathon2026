# Kaggle and public insurance claims / pricing competitions: what won, and what transfers to the IAA 2026 Part 1 task

Method note (read before using). Evidence tiers used below:
- READ = full text of the winner's or participant's own post, fetched through Kaggle's internal discussion API or raw.githubusercontent.com in this sandbox.
- META = competition metadata (number of teams, metric, public-leaderboard share, dates) read from Kaggle's competition API. Page URLs are given as the citation.
- SNIPPET = only seen in a search-result summary. The page was never opened, because kaggle.com and raw.githubusercontent.com were the only reachable hosts; AIcrowd, actuaries.digital, casact.org, Kaggle blog and Medium returned proxy 403 or DNS errors. Snippet items are marked and should not be treated as verified.
- Where the same post URL is cited several times, it is the same READ document.
- No source I found uses pooled Tweedie D2 with power 1.5 as the metric. Every "transfer" below is an inference.

## Q1. Which competitions are the closest analogues, and what were their metric, size, validation, winning models, target treatment, ensembling and post-processing?

### Takeaway
No Kaggle competition matches our task (next-year ultimate cost, Tweedie D2, per-peril, synthetic). The best structural analogues are (a) the AIcrowd Insurance Pricing Game winner, whose Tweedie-based stack and large-claim model are documented in a README, (b) Liberty Mutual Fire Peril (0.26% claim rate, heavy tail, weighted Gini), (c) Allstate Claims Severity (heavy-tailed severity, MAE), and (d) M5 (zero-inflated, Tweedie objective, time-based validation). Porto Seguro is a frequency-style, anonymised, Gini-scored task, mainly useful for validation and ensembling lessons. Allstate Purchase Prediction and AXA Telematics are not relevant and can be dropped.

### Cited Findings

**A. AIcrowd Insurance Pricing Game (2020), 1st place (davidlkl, Kai Leong Ling)**
- READ. The repo README says: features were binning (for the GLM; clipping implicit), interactions (population density, driver-gender combinations, vehicle value x weight, "present vehicle value" with exponential decay by age), grouping Med1 and Med2 into one policy type, log and power transforms, and history variables (historical claim amount, historical claim count, years since last claim, change in NCD). — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/davidlkl/Insurance-Pricing-Game/main/README.md)
- READ. Same README, "Large Claim detection model": an XGBoost plus a logistic regression to predict whether a claim exceeds 3k. — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/davidlkl/Insurance-Pricing-Game/main/README.md)
- READ. Same README, "Claim estimation model": 7 base models (Tweedie GLM, LightGBM, DeepForest, XGBoost, CatBoost, a neural net with Tweedie deviance as the loss, and a neural net with a log-normal likelihood learning mu and sigma), stacked with a Tweedie GLM as meta-learner under 5-fold CV. The pricing strategy lives in `predict_premium` in Model.py. — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/davidlkl/Insurance-Pricing-Game/main/README.md)
- SNIPPET (AIcrowd forum post, not opened): the winner said they changed little from their earlier model, added claim-history features and cut the profit loading by 1%. A std-based price (1.15 x E(claim) + 0.01 x Std(claim)) "was quite bad" and was dropped. — search result for [1st place solution](https://discourse.aicrowd.com/t/1st-place-solution/5199)
- Caveat: this game scored profit in a simulated market (cheapest price wins the customer), not prediction accuracy, so the pricing-strategy layer does not transfer. The modelling stack does.
- Metric, validation split and leaderboard shake-up for this game: not found.

**B. Liberty Mutual Group, Fire Peril Loss Cost (2014)**
- META. Weighted Gini, 632 teams, 14,758 submissions, 50% of test rows on the public board, 8 Jul to 2 Sep 2014. — [competition](https://www.kaggle.com/c/liberty-mutual-fire-peril)
- READ. A top-20 team (Jiwei Liu, ranked 18th in the forum metadata) blended xgboost, a neural net and linear models (BayesRidge, Lars, Ridge) for 0.307 private. They state the nnet and linear models "have serious overfitting": public/private 0.38/0.26 for nnet and 0.38/0.28 for linear, while the best xgb was 0.38/0.31 and was not even in the blend. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/liberty-mutual-fire-peril/discussion/10194)
- SNIPPET (CAS 2015 slides by DataRobot's Xavier Conort, not opened): DataRobot finished 1st of 634 teams, treated the data as high severity and low frequency, capped the target at the 80% quantile of non-zero losses (found more accurate and robust), downsampled non-claims, and used feature selection to reduce noise. A single source snippet also gives roughly 0.26% claim rate and 1,188 claims in 452,061 training policies. — search result for [CAS handout](https://casact.org/sites/default/files/presentation/rpm_2015_handouts_paper_3896_handout_2468_0.pdf)
- The forum has threads titled "Quantifying Leaderboard Shake-up", "Totally confused: How did I go from 100 to 43?" and "test set over-fitting". Their bodies are comment-only and were not retrievable. — [forum](https://www.kaggle.com/c/liberty-mutual-fire-peril/discussion)

**C. Liberty Mutual Group, Property Inspection Prediction (2015)**
- META. Normalized Gini, 2,232 teams, 45,786 submissions, 30% public. — [competition](https://www.kaggle.com/c/liberty-mutual-group-property-inspection-prediction)
- READ. Several participants report that plain XGBoost (linear, Poisson, or RMSE objectives) was as good as ensembles. One 81st-place entrant says an ensemble of 3 xgboost models would have scored 0.393273 private, better than his chosen KNN/RF/XGB blend at 0.392898, which would have meant rank about 55 instead of 81. Another says "no improvement by adding other model libraries". A top-15 entry (0.394519 private) was built on public stacked-generalisation and XGB scripts. — [My Solution](https://www.kaggle.com/c/liberty-mutual-group-property-inspection-prediction/discussion/16254), [Sharing my solution](https://www.kaggle.com/c/liberty-mutual-group-property-inspection-prediction/discussion/16401), [My Top15 Solution](https://www.kaggle.com/c/liberty-mutual-group-property-inspection-prediction/discussion/16318)
- The winners' own write-ups were not found. The target is an inspection hazard score, so it is a weak analogue.

**D. Allstate Claims Severity (2016)**
- META. MAE, 3,045 teams, 52,353 submissions, 30% public, 10 Oct to 12 Dec 2016. — [competition](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity)
- READ, 1st place (Bishwarup B). Best single model: XGBoost with the two-way interactions from the forum plus selected 3-, 4-, 5- and 7-way interactions chosen by mutual information, trained with the fair objective (constant 0.7) on a (1 + loss)^0.25 target; 10-fold CV about 1124, public 1105.12. Best NN trained on log(loss + shift), 10-fold x 10 bags. Also RGF, LightGBM, Vowpal Wabbit, LibFM, LibFFM, RF, ExtraTrees, glmnet. Tried count:poisson on rounded, log-transformed targets (public 1168, poor alone but added diversity), and XGB trained on bottom 5/10 percentile and top 70/80/90 percentile of the data "to get a better estimate of the exorbitant loss values". 81 first-level models; second level XGB plus two NNs; best L2 CV 1114.645, public 1097.87. Final = weighted average of power-transformed outputs `w1*NN1^w2 + w3*NN2^w4 + w5*XGB1^w6 + w7`, weights fit with Nelder-Mead in a one-fold manner then applied to test and averaged over 10 repeats, plus a piecewise correction on NN1 for low and high predictions. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26416)
- READ, 2nd place (self-described: 9th on public, 2nd on private). About 70 L1 models (XGB, Keras NN, LightGBM, sklearn models, LibFM), many data and target transformations; best XGB CV 1122.65 / public 1105.02 / private 1116.74. SVD and cluster-distance + RBF features helped non-tree models (linear regression CV 1237 to 1202). About 50 L2 models, grouped and averaged into 10 features for an L3 quantile regression (statsmodels), 8 bags per fold, power correction such as X^1.03 on some groups, then averaged around ten of his best submissions from different times. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26427)
- SNIPPET (Kaggle blog interview with the 2nd-place finisher, not opened): the interview credits cluster-distance features, SVD and categorical interactions, targets log(loss + 200) and loss^0.25, three stacking levels with bias correction and median regression at the top, public 1098.07 and private 1110.01. — search result for [interview](http://blog.kaggle.com/2017/02/27/allstate-claims-severity-competition-2nd-place-winners-interview-alexey-noskov/)
- READ, 3rd place (Faron). About 100 base models (70% XGB, 30% Keras); 5-fold unstratified CV with the same folds at base and 2nd level; many target transforms (y, log(y+n), sqrt, Box-Cox); XGB objectives including fair, reg:linear, gamma, logistic and Poisson on counts; NN with MAE or pinball loss; a 20-bag one-hidden-layer Keras stacker. Post-processing of a single XGB prediction as `yhat_opt = x1 * yhat**x2 + x3` improved CV from 1125.9 to 1124.1. He noted the Keras stacker removed the need for post-processing, and XGB as a stacker was about 3 MAE worse. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26447)
- READ, 7th place (Giba). Four base model types on many target transforms (t^1 to t^(1/8), log(t + 100/200/400), 10/t); blend weights by Nelder-Mead directly minimising MAE, also fit separately per prediction range ([0, 2000] vs above). He says this "worked very well with CV and public LB, but overfited a little Private LB". — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26537)
- READ, 8th place: loss 0.7 x FAIR + 0.15 x RMSE + 0.15 x MAE gave about 2 MAE better than FAIR alone; 17 base models; very low eta (0.0025, about 13K trees). — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26440)
- READ, a participant who dropped from public 1101.48 to private 1115.56 with a Ridge L2 over 40+ models asks why; in another post a mid-table entrant says he got "sloppy with blending" and thinks leakage in locally optimised blends was his biggest mistake. — [shock post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26449), [what worked](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26580)
- Note: the forum has a thread "xgboost now supports Tweedie Regression for insurance applications" (Nov 2016), but its body was not retrievable and no top solution reports using Tweedie. — [thread](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/25208)

**E. Porto Seguro's Safe Driver Prediction (2017)**
- META. Normalized Gini, 5,156 teams, 93,568 submissions, 30% public, 29 Sep to 29 Nov 2017. — [competition](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction)
- READ, 1st place (Michael Jahrer). Blend of 6 models (1 LightGBM on raw features, 5 NNs trained on denoising-autoencoder activations), all weights 1. "Nonlinear stacking failed, simple averaging works best", and even tuning linear weights failed. Features: dropped `*calc`, one-hot on `*cat`, 221 dense features, no imputation. DAE used swap noise (15% of cells replaced by values from other rows), trained on train + test features; blow-up to 1k-10k dimensions. Inputs RankGauss-normalised for NNs. 5-fold CV, fixed seed, each model own seed. He says 2 single models (#1 + #2) would have scored 0.29502 private and the full 6-model blend 0.2965; adding 32-bag versions of all six gave private 0.2969 and "was not really worth the effort". A control without test features in the DAE gave a similar private score (0.29235 vs 0.29298). Tried and failed: upsampling, deeper/wider autoencoders, KNN, PCA, GANs, bagging (small gain), nonlinear stacking, xgboost (added nothing to the blend). — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44629)
- READ, 2nd place team. NN 5-fold CV 0.294, public 0.284, private 0.290; the NN had embeddings on categoricals, interaction features, group-aggregates, count features, and "xgboost predictions of one feature group from the other two groups" as features; 2-layer relu with 0.8 dropout. LightGBM 0.29124 private. They "tried very hard on nonlinear stacking (without success)" and relied on weighted-average CV. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44558)
- READ, 3rd place: average of 1 LightGBM and 1 NN on the same feature space. Greedy feature elimination by lgb CV, one-hot encoding of categoricals (and of small-cardinality numerics for the NN), strongly regularised LightGBM (num_leaves 16, min_data_in_leaf 16, lambda_l1 10, bagging 0.5, lr 0.01). His own words: "almost everything that you will try ... will result in no significant change". — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44608)
- READ, entrant who moved from 1178th public to 29th private: 5 repeats of stratified 5-fold CV with different fold seeds, 3 seeds per fold (75 models) so that the averaged OOF Gini tracked the public board; heavily regularised XGBoost (`min_child_weight 6`, `gamma 10`, `alpha 16`, `scale_pos_weight 3`, `max_delta_step 1`); ridge with positive weights as blender, using the same folds as level 1 (different folds overfit); isolation forest/KNN/GMM "led to overfit". Reasoned that the public board "was no different" from one fixed fold and ignored it. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44614)
- READ, 18th place: 16-model stack with regularised logistic regression; sandwich down-sampling made a top-100 vs top-20 difference; adding models trained with heavy upsampling raised CV (0.2920 to 0.2924) and public LB but lowered private, and he says CV gain came from one fold only. Choosing his best LB 3 days earlier would have been 11th. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44579)
- READ, 12th place: 207 one-hot features, XGB + LightGBM + NN, harmonic-mean blend rather than stacking, blend weights 0.4/0.4/0.2. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44642)

### Inferences
- The two documented Tweedie-aware designs (IPG winner's stack; M5 GBMs) both treat the Tweedie objective as one family member rather than the whole solution. Our finding that a direct Tweedie GBM was not better than frequency x severity is consistent with this; a Tweedie GBM/NN may still earn a place as a diverse ensemble member.
- The IPG winner's separate >3k large-claim model and the Allstate winner's training on subsets of the loss distribution both address tail estimation. Our capped-Gamma severity is a related but different handling.
- Across Allstate, Porto and Fire Peril, winners used 6 to 100+ models, but the Porto winner states two models were sufficient and the Liberty 2015 entrants found single XGBoost objectives about as good as blends. Ensemble breadth beyond a few diverse members shows diminishing and sometimes negative returns.

## Q2. What distinguished the top 1-3 from the top 10, and what do these competitions teach about public-leaderboard overfitting with noisy heavy-tailed targets?

### Takeaway
In the heavy-tailed or low-signal competitions here, the leaderboard reshuffle was large, and winners mostly differed by trusting a variance-reduced local CV over the public score, by restraint in stacking (positive/regularised weights, simple averages), and by a few high-leverage design choices (target/loss handling, representation) rather than by sheer model count. Public-board gaps of the size separating our 0.1246 from 0.128 (about 0.0034) should be regarded as potentially within noise, but this is inference: no source reports that noise level for a Tweedie D2 metric.

### Cited Findings
- Porto Seguro: a participant at public rank 1178 finished 29th private. He ran 5x5-fold CV with different fold seeds and 3 seeds per fold, ignoring the public board because "it was no different" from a single fixed fold; he says he watched his rank sink by more than 1000 places in the final weeks and concluded that "people were overfitting to public LB in mass". — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44614)
- Porto Seguro: the 2nd-place team says they "did trust mostly on local CV" and only submitted when local CV improved; the 18th-place entrant says adding upsampled models improved CV, public LB, and worsened private, and that the CV improvement came from one fold. — [2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44558), [18th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44579)
- Porto Seguro winner: 1st place private about 0.2969 versus 0.29502 for his best two models and 0.2965 for the 6-model blend, so the winning margin over simpler variants was in the fourth decimal; his own words call the 32-bag expansion "not really worth the effort". — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44629)
- Fire Peril: one top-20 team found NN and linear models with public 0.38 fell to 0.26 and 0.28 private, while xgboost went 0.38 to 0.31. — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/liberty-mutual-fire-peril/discussion/10194)
- Allstate Claims Severity: 2nd place was 9th on public; a heavy blender (Giba, 7th) says Nelder-Mead MAE-optimised blend weights "overfited a little Private LB"; one participant reports dropping from public 1101.48 to private 1115.56; the 1st-place solution used the same Nelder-Mead blending but averaged weights over 10 repeated fits; the 2nd place averaged about ten of his own submissions and built an L3 with 8 bags to reduce noise. — [Giba](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26537), [shock](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26449), [1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26416), [2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26427)
- Allstate Claims Severity: an entrant who made two final submissions (best LB and best local CV) says the best-CV one scored best private and concludes "Trust your cross-validation!"; 3rd place also notes his best-CV model was also best on LB. — [My approach](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26411), [3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26447)
- Positive-weight/regularised blending was the repeated winner pattern: Porto 1st (all weights 1), Porto 29th-private entrant (ridge with positive weights, same folds as level 1), Porto 2nd (nonlinear stacking failed), Allstate entrant (L1 penalty on blend weights), Playground S4E12 2nd/9th (Ridge, hill-climb, Lasso). — see posts cited in Q1
- M5: winners avoided multipliers and used several time-based validation windows; 1st place selected on mean and std across windows because scores varied strongly by window. — [1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/m5-forecasting-accuracy/discussion/163684), [4th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/m5-forecasting-accuracy/discussion/163216)
- Playground S4E12 (low-noise, large public share relative to others): no top-10 shake-up reported by the 2nd place; the 1st place won with a single model but 611 engineered features, i.e. the edge was in the feature search, not the blend. — [2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s4e12/discussion/554505), [1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s4e12/discussion/554328)
- Public-board shares: Allstate Severity 30%, Porto 30%, Fire Peril 50%, Property Inspection 30%, S4E12 20%. — competition pages cited in Q1. Our task uses about 33%, comparable to Porto/Allstate.

### Inferences
- What separated top 1-3 from top 10 in these threads was seldom a single trick. Observable differences: (1) one high-leverage idea (Porto 1st: DAE + swap noise; Allstate 1st: interactions + fair loss + (1+y)^0.25 transform + very broad diversity; S4E12 1st: large categorical-encoding search), (2) disciplined regularisation of the blend, (3) CV-first selection and, in the extreme, no public-board optimisation.
- Models with high variance that look good on the public subset (NN/linear on Fire Peril, upsampled models on Porto) were the ones that lost; a GBM with strong regularisation was the more stable choice in these threads. For our heavy tail, any component whose gain appears only on the public 33% and not across several time-forward CV folds should be treated as noise.
- Because our metric is pooled over rows and dominated by a few very large claims, the effective sample size of the public subset is closer to the number of large claims in it than to the number of rows. That is an inference from the metric; I did not find a source quantifying it.

### Gaps
- No quantitative private-vs-public rank correlation for any of these competitions could be retrieved (leaderboard downloads not available through the route used).
- No evidence on shake-up for a Tweedie-type metric specifically. M5 used WRMSSE; Fire Peril and Porto used Gini; Allstate used MAE.

## Q3. Which techniques would plausibly add real score in our setting (heavy tail, zero-inflated, exposure offsets, per-peril structure, next-year extrapolation)?

### Takeaway
Strongest-supported, low-risk candidates: repeated multi-seed / multi-fold averaging of the existing LightGBM (variance reduction), time-forward multi-window validation with mean and std selection, a small number of diverse members (Tweedie-deviance NN, direct Tweedie GBM, the freq x sev GBM) combined with non-negative regularised or simple weights, a large-claim handling review (cap level, separate large-loss component), and a 2-3 parameter monotone calibration fit on forward OOF predictions. Higher-effort, less certain: leak-safe target/count encoding search for high-cardinality categoricals, DAE-style representation learning.

### Cited Findings
- Variance reduction by repeated CV and seed averaging: Porto 29th-private entrant (75 models per level), M5 1st (several window and recursive/non-recursive variants), Allstate 1st/2nd (10x repeated blend fits, bagging). Jahrer reports 32-bagging gave small gains (0.2965 to 0.2969 with other additions). — [Porto 29th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44614), [Porto 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44629)
- Diverse member types in the one documented Tweedie-aware winner: Tweedie GLM, LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, DeepForest, a Tweedie-deviance NN, and a log-normal-likelihood NN (mu and sigma), meta-learner a Tweedie GLM under 5-fold CV, plus a separate >3k large-claim classifier (XGBoost and logistic) and history features (historical claim amount/count, years since last claim, NCD change). — [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/davidlkl/Insurance-Pricing-Game/main/README.md)
- Capping the target at the 80% quantile of non-zero losses (Fire Peril 1st) — SNIPPET only, from a [CAS handout](https://casact.org/sites/default/files/presentation/rpm_2015_handouts_paper_3896_handout_2468_0.pdf) search result; unverified.
- Level / calibration post-processing has mixed evidence: Allstate 3rd place got a CV gain from `x1 * yhat**x2 + x3` on a single model but a bagged Keras stacker removed the gain; Allstate 1st fitted power-transformed weights and piecewise corrections; M5 1st and 4th deliberately did not use multipliers, M5 2nd used a custom asymmetric loss and a top-level alignment instead. — [Allstate 3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26447), [Allstate 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/allstate-claims-severity/discussion/26416), [M5 1st](https://www.kaggle.com/c/m5-forecasting-accuracy/discussion/163684), [M5 2nd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/m5-forecasting-accuracy/discussion/164599)
- Heavy regularisation for weak-signal binary targets: Porto 3rd and the 29th-private entrant used shallow trees, large `min_child_weight`/`min_data_in_leaf`, L1/L2, and set `min_child_weight` so a leaf must "isolate at least two claims". — [3rd](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44608), [29th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/porto-seguro-safe-driver-prediction/discussion/44614)
- Feature work that mattered where reported: interactions chosen by mutual information or by Xgbfi on a first model (Allstate 1st/2nd); target-encoding and count-encoding families plus brute-force combination search (S4E12 1st; gain CV about 1.03 to 1.016); aggregate/count features and models of one feature group from others (Porto 2nd); unsupervised representation learning with swap-noise DAE (Porto 1st, flagged by Jahrer as especially suited to anonymised data, and by a commentator in a search snippet); history and no-claim-discount features in the IPG winner.
- Separate models per segment (store, store x category) in M5 1st — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/m5-forecasting-accuracy/discussion/163684). Our per-peril split is the analogue.
- Using original/source data to augment training (S4E12 2nd, CV 1.031 to 1.027) — [post](https://www.kaggle.com/c/playground-series-s4e12/discussion/554505). In our case the analogue would be nothing external, as the data is synthetic; flagged as not transferable.
- Failed or neutral techniques reported: nonlinear stacking (Porto 1st, 2nd, 29th), isolation forest/KNN/GMM anomaly features (Porto 29th, though a 0.23 Gini from isolation forest alone was noted), upsampling to majority (Porto 18th), GANs (Porto 1st), XGB as stacker (Allstate 3rd), lambda-rank (Porto 29th), WRMSSE-as-loss and classification loss (M5 3rd). — posts cited in Q1

### Inferences
Ranked by expected value for our task, with risk:
1. Repeated K-fold x multi-seed averaging of the final per-peril LightGBMs, and selection of every decision by the mean and standard deviation across several forward-in-time folds (2022 to 2024 as validation years), not the public board. Low cost, repeatedly cited as the most reliable edge; expected gain is small but real and reduces the risk of a public-board-only improvement.
2. Ensemble of 3-5 deliberately different members and a non-negative regularised blender (or equal weights): current freq x sev GBM, a direct Tweedie-p1.5 GBM (tested "not better" alone but a different error pattern), and ideally a Tweedie-deviance NN per the IPG winner. Evaluate blending on forward-time OOF with the same fold structure at both levels (the Porto 29th entrant found mismatched folds overfit). Avoid XGB/GBM stackers and unconstrained weights.
3. Re-examine the tail: tune the severity cap level by forward CV on the actual pooled Tweedie D2, consider a separate large-claim probability or large-loss component (IPG winner's >3k model; Allstate 1st trained on percentile subsets), and check whether the capped-claim remainder is restored through a global scale rather than per-row. Fire Peril's 80%-quantile cap is unverified.
4. A tiny monotone calibration `a * yhat^b` (and/or per-peril scale) fit on forward-time OOF, evaluated by D2 under the same forward CV. Evidence is mixed (3rd-place Allstate gain; M5 winners avoided multipliers); since next-year extrapolation (trend, completeness) is the main source of level error in our setting, this is plausibly useful but must be judged only on out-of-time folds, never the public board.
5. History features (previous claims count and amount, years since last claim, NCD changes, customer/address history) were central in the IPG winner; our team already tested customer/address history, so only verify coverage of the specific set (claim count, years since last claim, NCD/bonus-malus change).
6. Medium effort, uncertain: leak-safe nested target and count encodings for high-cardinality categoricals, plus searched combinations (S4E12 1st). That competition had a different target and no time shift, so transfer to a forward-in-time claims setting is untested.
7. Low priority: DAE / swap-noise representation, GAN-like methods, extreme model counts. Evidence of gain is for anonymised tabular classification, not for exposure-offset Tweedie.
- Regarding the stated gap to the top (0.1246 vs 0.128 on the public third): based on the shake-up evidence, a fair amount of that gap may not persist on the private score, and conversely, our own public score is equally uncertain. Choose final submissions by forward-CV mean and std, optionally submitting one best-CV and one most-regularised variant as in Allstate and Porto reports.

### Gaps
- No competition in this review had an exposure offset or a per-peril structure; transfer of the exposure and per-peril points is by analogy to M5's per-store models and the IPG winner's policy-level Tweedie stack only.
- No tested evidence for Tweedie power selection (1.1 to 1.9) from winners: M5 posts state "tweedie" without reporting the variance power in the posts I read. Not verified.
- Nothing retrieved on how winners estimated or validated trend/inflation extrapolation, since none of these tasks scored a future year with development lag.
- Documented ideas relying on SNIPPET evidence (DataRobot 80% cap, Noskov interview details, Actuarial Loss Prediction winner) need confirmation from the original documents before adoption.
